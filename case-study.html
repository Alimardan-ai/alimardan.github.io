Case Study

Personal Business Website
for a Local Client

A friend running a small freelance photography business needed an online presence fast. I stepped in as the project manager, coordinating the brief, the designer, and the developer to deliver a clean, functional website in under five weeks.

Client

Independent photographer

Duration

5 weeks

Role

Project Manager

Status

Delivered

The brief

The client had been operating entirely through word-of-mouth and needed a professional website to attract new clients, showcase their portfolio, and include a booking form. The budget was limited, the timeline was tight, and the client had no technical knowledge which meant clear communication was everything.

Project timeline

Week 1

Discovery & briefing

Ran a kickoff meeting with the client to define goals, audience, and must-have features. Documented everything into a clear brief for the team.

Week 2

Design phase

Coordinated with the designer on layout and visual direction. Presented two concepts to the client, gathered feedback, and locked in the final direction.

Week 3–4

Development

Managed the developer's workload, tracked progress daily, and acted as the bridge between client feedback and technical implementation. Kept scope from expanding.

Week 5

Review & delivery

Ran a final review session with the client, collected last-minute changes, and oversaw the live launch. Delivered on time with zero scope creep.

What I learned

Scope creep is silent. The client kept adding small requests that individually seemed harmless. I learned to document every addition and flag its impact on the timeline before agreeing.
Non-technical clients need translation, not jargon. Every update I gave was framed around outcomes, not tasks "the booking form is ready for your review" not "the backend integration is complete."
Structure creates speed. The clearer the brief at the start, the fewer revisions at the end. Week 1 saved us two weeks later.
